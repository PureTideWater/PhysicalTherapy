### S1. [should_fix] A client past its own bounce budget can test any address against the refusal list, with nothing recorded and nothing sent

- **Where:** packages/db/migrations/20261107090000_mail_streams.sql:279-306 (order stated at 30-36); apps/web/src/lib/screening/routes.ts:785-788; packages/db/src/screening.db.test.ts:1506-1507
- **Failure path:** 1. An attacker earns 3 live client bounce marks from one IP. They send three of their own screenings to mailboxes on a domain they control that rejects the message permanently. The address check lets these through: validation says MEDIUM and keepAddress=1, or the day's cap is spent. 2. For the next 7 days, every submission from that IP that names a target address and passes the row checks and the rate limit gets one of two answers. 2a. If the target is on screening_mail_refusal, the BEFORE INSERT trigger on screening_upload raises screening_contact_undeliverable, and the form shows ADDRESS_REFUSED on contactEmail. 2b. If not, the upload goes in, and the later BEFORE INSERT trigger on screening_run raises screening_bounce_budget, and the form shows SCREENING_BOUNCE_BUDGET on input. 3. Both answers roll back: no prospect, upload, run or audit row is kept, and no email reaches the target. The attacker learns whether the address bounced permanently, or reported a screening as spam, in the last 12 months. The target is never told. The limit is 10 probes an hour per IP and 20 a day per account. Known item (b) assumes that a probe which is not refused emails the target; this variant needs no email at all.
- **Evidence:** I added a temporary case to packages/db/src/screening.db.test.ts in my clone. It gives one client subject 3 marks (markSubject), puts one address on the refusal list and leaves another off, and calls submit() with that clientSubject for each. Result: the listed address gave 'screening_contact_undeliverable' and the unlisted one gave 'screening_bounce_budget'. The combined count of screening_prospect, screening_upload and screening_run rows was unchanged. The whole file passed, 69 of 69, and I then restored it. The PR's own D2 test asserts the same with the account subject at screening.db.test.ts:1506-1507.
- **Fix:** Refuse on the submitter before the address. Check the bounce budget, on both the client and account subjects, before the refusal list. For example, use one BEFORE INSERT trigger on screening_run that checks the budget first and then the hash of the upload's prospect address, and drop the hash lookup from screening_upload_mail_gate (keep the pause check there). D2's order becomes: closed; rows; screening_paused; screening_bounce_budget; screening_contact_undeliverable. Update the migration header, ADR 0065 and the D2 test. Add a test that a client past its budget gets screening_bounce_budget for an address on the list. The migration is unmerged, so edit it through the new-migration marker route and regenerate the domain model.

### S2. [should_fix] The refusal list is keyed by the exact spelling, so another spelling of the same inbox is mailed again after a complaint or bounce

- **Where:** packages/db/migrations/20261107090000_mail_streams.sql:94-97 (lower(btrim(address))), 286-287 (gate), 985-988 (event); compare apps/web/src/lib/screening/rate.ts:105-134
- **Failure path:** 1. A prospect's address jane.doe@gmail.com reports a screening result as spam, and its keyed hash goes on the refusal list for 365 days. 2. A new screening names janedoe@gmail.com, jane.doe+screening@gmail.com or jane.doe@googlemail.com. All of these reach the same inbox. 3. Each one is accepted and emailed, so the person who reported spam gets screening mail again within 12 months. That is what Q11 (b) keeps the list for. 4. A complaint adds no bounce mark, so no budget stops this. Each new complaint counts toward the stream's cut-off: three complaints under 2,500 sends pause the screening for everyone. One mailbox under its +tag spellings can do that again after every resume. A permanently bouncing mailbox is likewise re-mailed under other spellings until the account budget catches it.
- **Evidence:** On a fresh migrated PostgreSQL 18 database (tp_pr6b4_review_security_1), I ran operator_record_mail_event as tp_operator for a screening_result complaint (abuse) to jane.doe@gmail.com, which wrote one refusal row. As tp_owner I then inserted accepted screening_upload rows for prospects at each spelling. jane.doe@gmail.com was refused (screening_contact_undeliverable). janedoe@gmail.com, jane.doe+screening@gmail.com and jane.doe@googlemail.com were all accepted. The rate limiter already counts these spellings as one mailbox (screeningRateAccount, rate.ts:119-134: googlemail.com read as gmail.com, the +tag dropped, Gmail's dots dropped).
- **Fix:** Hash the canonical mailbox, not the spelling. Canonicalize inside mail_recipient_sha256(), so the upload gate and the event path agree: lower-case and trim, drop a domain's trailing dots, read googlemail.com as gmail.com, drop Gmail's dots, and drop a +tag. Drop the +tag at every domain, not only free-mail ones: Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Fastmail, Proton and iCloud all deliver sub-addresses to the base mailbox, and refusing a rare distinct +mailbox costs little. Hold the rules in one place, with a unit test that the SQL and TypeScript forms agree on a table of spellings. Add a db test that every spelling is refused after one complaint and after one permanent bounce. The screening has not launched, so there are no stored hashes to migrate.

### S3. [should_fix] The account trail very likely records every address SES validates, and keeps it indefinitely

- **Where:** apps/web/src/lib/screening/validation.ts:76-79; infra/lib/constructs/account-security.ts:81-90; docs/policies/data-retention.md:37; apps/worker/src/platform/mail-events.ts:36-37
- **Failure path:** 1. Each screening submission that passes the row checks and the rate limit calls SESv2 GetEmailAddressInsights from the web task role, with EmailAddress set to the prospect's address (up to 1,000 a day in production). 2. This is not a send operation, and SES's CloudTrail data events cover only the sends, so it is a read-only management event. 3. The account trail records management events with ReadWriteType All. It writes them to a versioned bucket with no lifecycle rule, and with removal policy RETAIN. 4. The SESv2 model does not mark this request's EmailAddress as sensitive, so CloudTrail's requestParameters would hold the address as typed. 5. Anyone with s3:GetObject on the trail bucket could then read every validated prospect address indefinitely, and anyone with cloudtrail:LookupEvents for 90 days. That contradicts data-retention.md:37 ('No other copy of a screening address is kept by the mail path'), mail-events.ts:36-37 ('Outside SES the address's only copy is the queue'), the screening's 30-day deletion promise and Q11 (b)'s keyed-hash-only rule. Caveat: I could not confirm it against a live trail, because AWS's documentation hosts are unreachable from this container.
- **Evidence:** From cdk synth of tp-production: AccountSecurityTrail has EventSelectors [{IncludeManagementEvents: true, ReadWriteType: All}]. AccountSecurityTrailBucket has VersioningConfiguration Enabled and LifecycleConfiguration absent. In @aws-sdk/client-sesv2 3.1152.0, dist-es/schemas/schemas_0.js:1646-1650 types GetEmailAddressInsightsRequest's EmailAddress as 0, a plain string. InsightsEmailAddress at :932, used by the other insights APIs, is typed 8, which is sensitive and which CloudTrail redacts.
- **Fix:** First, verify on staging after phase 2, and add this to O11 now: make one validation, then run `aws cloudtrail lookup-events --lookup-attributes AttributeKey=EventName,AttributeValue=GetEmailAddressInsights` and read requestParameters. If the address is there, CloudTrail cannot exclude this one read API: advanced selectors for management events filter only readOnly, and eventSource only for KMS and RDS Data. The best fix is to make the call from a dedicated validation account. The web task would assume a role there that allows only ses:GetEmailAddressInsights, and that account's trail would record write management events only; the main trail stays as it is. Keep the screening closed until this is settled (counsel, O4). Correct data-retention.md:37 and mail-events.ts:36-37 to whatever is true.

### M2. [should_fix] Bounces from SES's global suppression list (Permanent/Suppressed) are recorded as 'suppressed' and left out of the cut-off, the refusal list and the bounce budget, although SES counts them toward the account's bounce rate

### Known items

- **(a) a completion committing just before a concurrent pause still sends** (real: True): Real and bounded. Sequence: consumer thread T runs completeRun's UPDATE at t0. screening_run_mail_gate reads screening_mail_sending() under that statement's snapshot, without locking mail_stream. At t1 the pause commits, from the console or from the cut-off. At t2 T's SendEmail is accepted through the still-enabled set: a cut-off pause disables the set right after it commits, a console pause only at the next mail_events run. At t3 T commits. The send is inside the completion's transaction, so this happens only to completions already past their UPDATE when the pause commits: at most one email per consumer thread (3), and likewise for failures in flight. After the pause commits, no claim, completion or failure passes the trigger, so this is not 'a minute of sending'. Best option: accept it and record the bound in ADR 0065. These are results owed to uploads accepted before the pause, and taking FOR SHARE on mail_stream in the trigger would hold a lock across the SES call and buy no reputation.
- **(c) a failure notice sent after the failure commits is lost to a pause in between** (real: True): Real, with a narrow window.

## What changes

- A new ROI statement stores, beside its actionable figure, the priced lines that figure counted
  (`links.actionable_open_lines`). The database refuses a new statement that carries the list unless its actionable
  figure is exactly the variance of the lines listed, each listed once and each a line of the practice on a case the
  statement lists. So the figure on a statement can be rebuilt from the statement itself after its cases have moved on.
- One migration, `20261115090000_roi_statement_actionable_lines.sql`, replaces the bodies of two database functions,
  `roi_links_valid` and `roi_statement_rules`, with the two texts the founder read and granted, byte for byte. It
  changes no table, column, constraint text, index, policy, trigger definition, grant or stored row.
- The statement writer (`generateRoiStatement`) reads each open case's figure and its lines in one query and one pass
  over the case's lines, and stores the list. The figure it stores is the figure it stored before, to the cent.
- The statement's page, CSV, list and print job select the four keys they read, so a long list is never sent to
  them. A person sees no word, figure or layout change.
- Statements already stored (four keys) stay valid and are never checked again.
- Records: ADR 0082's amendment of 2026-11-13; `docs/product/reconciliation-001.md` ("Ledger semantics": the grant and
  the "Amended" line); the deploy runbook's section "An ROI statement's listed lines"; the data dictionary's
  `roi_statement` row; test matrix LED-26, LED-27 and LED-28 with their registry entries.

## What the founder should know before approving

1. **The migration's check of stored statements takes one short SHARE lock; it does not take "only reading locks".**
   The ask you granted said the preflight "takes only reading locks". That sentence was wrong and is corrected here.
   Before the migration replaces the second function it reads every stored statement through the new rule, then, for
   its closing count, takes a SHARE lock on the statements table and holds it to the end of the migration. While it
   is held, anything writing a statement waits. Measured on 1,000,000 stored statements in 1,000 practices (synthetic
   rows): the closing count under the lock took **0.16 to 2.77 seconds**. When a writer's transaction was open for 20
   seconds across the closing, the lock **waited 16 seconds** for it and the migration then finished. When the lock
   could never be had (a writer open past the migration's 30 tries), the migration **refused at 219.7 seconds with
   nothing changed**, and the deploy is simply run again. On an empty table, which is what every real database has
   today, all of this takes milliseconds. Lane A's orchestrator approved the lock on 2026-11-12 (the complete proof
   being the safer option), under the co-founder's standing instruction; you may overrule it.
2. **The 5-minute limit is met with the data already in memory, and was not measured on cold storage.** At 1,000,000
   statements the whole migration took **184 to 196 seconds** with pages cached. It was not run against storage that
   had to be read from disk first. This is a recorded residual; no real database holds a statement yet.
3. **A refusal prints counts only.** If a stored statement fails the new rule, the migration stops, changes nothing,
   and says how many statements in how many practices failed. It prints no statement, practice or line identifier.
4. **The writer's cost at a large month, before and after.** For a practice with 30,795 lines in 1,200 open cases
   (synthetic), the writer's query took **3.6 to 4.0 seconds before this change and takes 3.4 to 3.8 seconds after**
   (medians, in the two planner states measured). The database's new check adds about half a second when the
   statement is stored (0.40 to 0.85 seconds across every state measured), so writing the statement is about a third
   of a second slower in all. The first build of the writer read each line twice and took 7.0 to 7.7 seconds; it was
   replaced by the one-pass form the design gave for that case, after both were shown to produce identical output
   (every case, cent for cent and line for line, on the fixture worlds and on generated months of 10,000 and 31,000
   lines). A statement listing 1,000,000 lines would take 28 to 29 seconds to check; no practice is near that.
5. **What the database's rule does not check, on record.** It checks that the figure is the sum of the lines listed
   and that each is a line of a listed case. It does not check that those were the right lines to count: a statement
   listing a written-off or stale line of a listed case, with a figure matching its list, is accepted. The writer is
   what chooses the lines, and tests hold it to the one predicate every other actionable figure reads.
6. **What is granted, and by which record.** Your exception of 2026-11-07 (relayed by the co-founder at 19:26 UTC:
   questions Q1, Q2 and Q3, each yes) and of 2026-11-12 (relayed at about 14:20 UTC: Appendix D2 in place of Appendix
   D, the same rule read one listed line at a time, and one header line on that function, `SET plan_cache_mode TO
   'force_custom_plan'`). It covers exactly Appendix A (`roi_links_valid`, body `13c4dd46b9203095`) and Appendix D2
   (`roi_statement_rules`, body `ad925199131cb411`) with that header line, word for word. It is recorded in
   `docs/product/reconciliation-001.md`, "Ledger semantics", with the questions as put. The migration refuses unless
   the bodies it replaces are the ones you granted the exception on and the bodies it stores hash to those two. The
   domain model's function appendix prints the same two hashes.
7. **Things a reader of the diff should expect.** A trigger function on a ledger table now reads `priced_line` and
   `case_line` as the caller (Q2). An IMMUTABLE function that a CHECK calls changes what it accepts, which is why the
   migration reads the stored rows first. The migration itself calls `set_config('app.practice_ids', ...)`,
   transaction-local, at four places (to read each practice's statements under the tenant policy, then all of them for
   the closing count, then to leave the scope empty); no function gains a `SET` other than the granted header
   line, none is SECURITY DEFINER, and no grant, owner, volatility or overload changes.
8. **Not done here.** The statement's page and PDF still say the actionable figure is "not recomputed from its
   links": the reader that would recompute it is not in this pull request. End-to-end browser tests were not run
   locally; CI runs them.

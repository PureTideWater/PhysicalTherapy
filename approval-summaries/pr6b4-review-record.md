## 2. What changes, why, and what it does not change

### What changes

PR 6b-4, the last of PR 6b (audit #76): mail streams, delivery recorded by message, and the screening's cut-off. This head adds the verification round of 2026-10-27 (three adversarial reviews of c37fa7c: security, the mail path, UX and brand; 19 findings, all real, all fixed, section 4), the M3 follow-up it reviewed, and the base merges of #76, #77, #80 and #79, on top of the review round of 2026-10-23 and the base merge of e908c21. Since the verification round (head be63bdc2): the base merges of #74 (89d33579, the forms that keep what the person typed) and #81 (17e233f0, triage and the audit report reading a month in batches), both clean, and the `images` job's fix ported from #86 (bd589960, be63bdc2), section 9.

- **Three SES streams.** Every message has one recipient and goes through one SES configuration set, `tp-<env>-transactional` (Cognito's mail from phase 2, the digests), `tp-<env>-notices` (support-access and dunning notices) or `tp-<env>-screening` (the screening's results and failure notices). Its identity travels as SES tags of opaque IDs and codes, never an address. No set tracks opens or clicks; the screening set keeps no SES suppression list.
- **Delivery recorded by message.** SES's events reach an encrypted queue (with its dead-letter queue) through EventBridge; the platform's `mail_events` job (every minute, as `tp_operator`) records each event once per message in any order (`mail_send`, no address), deletes it only after the record commits, keeps the screening set's sending in step with its stream, reads the cut-off on the window as it stands, and sends probes to SES's simulator only. A message SES held back for the account's own suppression list, whether SES reports it as a bounce or as a complaint, reads not delivered; a bounce from SES's global suppression list counts as a hard bounce, as SES counts it.
- **Probes that page and stay raised.** A stream is missed once the older of its delivered and bounced probe since the last resume is 26 hours old (a lost daily probe pages 2 hours after it went, 22 before readiness lapses), or, with one not back since a resume or an environment's first activation, an hour after the first probe sent since then, which a re-send never moves. `MAIL_PROBE_MISSED` is logged on every run until both come back, and a missed stream's probes go hourly, so a fixed event path reopens the screening within the hour. Each probe event is logged by its SES message ID and codes, which the staging checks read.
- **Notices from a recorded outbox.** Support-access (break-glass) and dunning notices are recorded per recipient, then sent one message per recipient in its own transaction, retried after 1, 5, 15 and 60 minutes. The grant page, the console's support list and an in-app notification say which notice reached whom. The users page words an undelivered invitation by its reason: bounced or rejected, check the address and invite again; reported as spam, find it in the spam folder; held back by SES's suppression list, its own sentence. Neither of the last two offers "Send again", whose resend would void the temporary password of the email the person may still find; both name PaidWhole's support, who releases the address and sends it from the console. Below the `sm` breakpoint the invitations are a list, so the page no longer scrolls sideways at 390 px.
- **The screening's cut-off (Q9).** In a 7-day window since the last resume, the screening stream alone pauses at 3% permanent bounces (floor 200 sends), 0.08% complaints other than not_spam (floor 2,500), half of SES's account review levels, or SES's review of the account. A platform administrator resumes it in `/console/mail`, with a reason; the page states the window as the later of 7 days ago and the last resume, its figures in the mono face, and that a warning still raised does not pause it again. While paused the page is closed and says so, the database refuses new uploads and every claim, completion and failure, and queued runs wait; a run priced whole on its last attempt is completed after the resume, and a failure and its notice go in one transaction.
- **Address checks (Q1 (g)).** A typo hint first; then the database's refusal for the submitter (closed, paused, its bounce budget), so no validation is spent on an upload it refuses for the submitter; then DNS and SES's validation together, at most one 2-second timeout. The refusal list answers only after all of them, from the run's trigger, so no refusal that ends without an email tells a listed address from another. A refusal of the upload as a whole is the alert's own text, with "request an audit" linked. SES's validation runs as a role in an AWS account of its own per environment, so no trail of the environment's account records the address (S3, section 7).
- **Refusal list and bounce budget (Q11 (b), Q10 (a)).** A keyed hash of the mailbox an address reaches (its spellings alike: ASCII case and white space, a +tag, Gmail's dots, googlemail.com, and me.com and mac.com for icloud.com; an address with any character outside ASCII stands as read), kept 365 days only to refuse another screening to it. A permanent bounce is keyed with its +tag kept (but at gmail.com), so a +tag a server does not deliver refuses that spelling alone, while a bounce of the base address refuses every +tag. 7-day bounce marks on the run's client and account subjects, refused at 3, and refused before the address is read. Every screening surface that says the address is deleted, the result email included, also says the coded form is kept 12 months and that the mail service keeps its record of the address check 90 days (awaiting the founder's sign-off, section 1).
- **Infrastructure.** Both identities verified in two phases behind `MailIdentitiesVerified`; the SQS interface endpoint; 17 mail alarms (`MAIL_PROBE_MISSED`'s description now says when it pages and that it stays raised); SES grants narrowed per set and identity, the probes' statement also requiring `ses:Recipients`; a new stack `tp-<env>-address-validation` for each environment's address-validation account, with the rule `ReleaseNeedsAddressValidationRole`.
- **Capacity (G1).** The harness charges the address checks' worst case (2,000 ms) to every upload under the unchanged 48-s line, reads the time the web measured for each upload's checks (`screening address checked`, the run's ID and `elapsed_ms`) and fails one with no such line or under 2,000 ms.
- **Docs.** ADR 0065 (new); ADRs 0062, 0049, 0055, 0050, 0019 and 0014 amended; reconciliation-001; `docs/runbooks/deliverability.md` (new: the staging checks read each probe's outcome by message ID, and a new check that a send through a disabled set is refused as paused; 3.4 gives each invitation reason's step and support's release) and the deploy runbook; `data-retention.md`; `legal-surfaces.md`; test matrix SCR-21, SCR-22, SEC-18, SEC-19 and JOB-05.

## 7. S3: address validation in its own account, and what remains

SES's `GetEmailAddressInsights` names the address in its request, and AWS records API calls. Re-checked on 2026-10-27 against AWS's own API models and reference text (AWS's documentation hosts cannot be reached from the build; staging confirms): every account keeps 90 days of management events, reads and writes, in CloudTrail's event history, which nothing turns off or shortens; SESv2 does not mark this request's `EmailAddress` sensitive; this call is a management event; and a trail's selectors filter management events only by read or write (and leave out only KMS's or RDS Data's), so no selector can leave out just this call, while CloudTrail Lake, which filters by event name, is closed to new customers.

Built, the safer option: each environment gets an AWS account of its own holding one role (assumable only by the environment's application role, allowed `ses:GetEmailAddressInsights` alone) and a trail of write events alone; the environment's application role is denied that call and the assuming of any role in its own account; the web holds a 15-minute session inside its 2-second bound; a refused validation pages (`MAIL_VALIDATION_REFUSED`); a release is refused until `MailValidationRoleArn` names the role.

What remains (ADR 0065, the deliverability runbook and `data-retention.md`): (1) that account's event history keeps each request, the address in it unless CloudTrail withholds it, for 90 days, longer than the screening's 30 and outside Q11 (b)'s hash-only rule: since the verification round the pages and the result email say so (`ADDRESS_CHECK_KEPT`, awaiting the founder's sign-off), and counsel sees it before the screening opens (O4); (2) the write-only trail keeps no validation only if CloudTrail reads the call as a read, which staging confirms (O11), else the screening stays closed and the fallback is no trail there; (3) a later reads trail, a log copy or an Organizations trail would keep addresses again, and the runbook forbids each; (4) the main account's trail records only the `AssumeRole` call (the role and session, never an address).
